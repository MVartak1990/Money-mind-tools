Roth vs traditional, TFSA vs RRSP, ISA vs pension: one decision under three names. If your tax rate is the same when money goes in and when it comes out, both accounts leave you exactly the same. So the number that decides it is your rate now vs your rate later. Enter yours to see which account leaves you more after tax. The math works in any currency.

1Your accounts

Any employer match comes first: it's free money, whichever account it lands in. The calculator then compares the rest.

2What you set aside

Enter it as pay before tax. The tax-free account gets what's left after tax; the tax-deferred account gets the full amount, because tax is skipped on the way in.

Same for both accounts.

3Your tax rate now and later

Use your marginal rate now: the bracket your last dollar lands in, not your average rate (total tax ÷ income). A deduction saves tax at your top rate. In retirement, withdrawals fill the brackets from the bottom up, so use the average rate you expect on them.

Income tax only. Add clawbacks below.

4The refund

The tax-deferred account only matches the tax-free one if the tax you save now (the refund, or the bump in take-home pay) is invested too. In a study of 11 US companies that added a Roth option, people kept saving the same percentage of pay whichever account they used (Beshears, Choi, Laibson & Madrian, 2017).

Verdict
—

How it works: tax-free = (pay × (1 − rate now)) grown, no tax at the exit. Tax-deferred = full pay grown, then taxed at your effective rate later (if you invest the refund); if the refund is spent, it gets the same after-tax amount as the tax-free account. Yearly amounts go in at the start of each year. Same return for both; fees, inflation and contribution limits are not modelled. Amounts are rounded to whole units.
